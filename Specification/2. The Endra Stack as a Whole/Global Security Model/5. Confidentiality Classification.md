## Confidentiality Classification

Data handled by the system is assigned a **confidentiality classification** according to the consequences of its disclosure to unauthorised parties.

The confidentiality classification describes the sensitivity of the data.
This classification is distinct from how strongly the data is secured.

### Exposed

**Exposed** data is data whose disclosure to an outside observer is acceptable.

Systems need not attempt to prevent outside observers from obtaining Exposed data.

Examples include:

- a peer's IP address
- the IP address of a device participating in the network

### Discreet

**Discreet** data is data whose disclosure to an unauthorised party is undesirable and considered metadata leakage, but is not expected to directly result in identity compromise or unauthorised access to user-level data.

Systems SHOULD prevent outside observers from obtaining Discreet data through the normal operation of the protocol.

Disclosure of Discreet data may reveal information about a user, identity, or system state, but does not by itself provide the attacker with the secrets required to impersonate an identity or access Secret data.

Examples include:

- identity identifiers
- identifiers of private communication networks
- metadata describing the existence or membership of an identity

### Secret

**Secret** data is data whose disclosure to an unauthorised party constitutes a direct risk of identity compromise or unauthorised access to user-level data.

Secret data MUST be protected against unauthorised disclosure using the strongest protection mechanisms required by this specification.

Examples include:

- private cryptographic keys
- plaintext user-level data such as private messages, where disclosure would constitute a direct breach of confidentiality
